When buying a bond, PV is negative; coupons and face value are positive. A calculator-style PV therefore has the opposite sign from the quoted bond price.

PV + PMT × A × timing + FV × D = 0

D = (1 + r)−N
A = (1 − D) / r, or N when r = 0.
r = I/Y ÷ 100 ÷ P/Y. Timing is 1 for END and (1 + r) for BGN.

How to use the five-input worksheet
  • Price: choose PV, then enter the total periods, annual yield, coupon per period, and face value.
  • Yield to maturity: choose I/Y and enter the purchase price as a negative PV. I/Y is a nominal annual yield; effective annual yield is different.
  • Frequency: 10 years with semiannual coupons means N = 20 and P/Y = 2. A 5% coupon on $1,000 means PMT = $25.
  • Zero-coupon bond: set PMT = 0. For a loan, PV is usually positive, PMT negative, and FV = 0.
  • Scope: fixed, equally spaced payments with matching payment and compounding frequencies. Bond examples assume settlement on a coupon date; accrued interest, calendar day counts, and callable-bond features are not included.
  • N can be fractional for a general TVM calculation. Regular coupon bonds use whole coupon periods. N is limited to 10,000.
  • Yield solving requires a conventional cash-flow pattern: PV has the opposite sign from PMT and FV. The numerical search supports per-period yields from −99.99% to 1,000,000%.

    Timing, rate conventions & interpreting IRR
    • The worksheet uses equally spaced periods. It is an NPV / IRR calculator, not XNPV / XIRR for irregular dates.
    • The entered discount rate and reported IRR are per period. For monthly cash flows, enter a monthly discount rate.
    • Effective annual IRR = (1 + periodic IRR)periods per year − 1. Changing periods per year does not change periodic NPV or IRR.
    • Repeat expands a cash-flow row into consecutive equal payments. Up to 100 groups and 600 future periods are supported.
    • IRR needs at least one inflow and one outflow. Multiple sign changes can produce multiple IRRs or no IRR. All numerically detected roots in the supported range are shown; do not treat one as a unique answer when several are listed.
    • The IRR search range is −99.99% to 1,000,000% per period. Repeated or nearly repeated roots are numerically sensitive. “No IRR found” means no root was detected in that range.
    • If all cash flows are zero, IRR is indeterminate: every discount rate produces zero NPV.
