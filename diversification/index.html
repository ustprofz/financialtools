Portfolio Theory Formulas & Definitions

Variable Definitions:

  • $w_S, w_B$: The proportionate weights of the stock and bond in the portfolio ($w_S + w_B = 1$).
  • $E(r_S), E(r_B)$: The expected rates of return for the stock and bond indexes.
  • $\sigma_S, \sigma_B$: The standard deviation (risk) of the stock and bond returns.
  • $\rho$: The correlation coefficient between the stock and bond returns (ranging from -1 to 1).
  • $Cov(r_S, r_B)$: The covariance between the two assets. It is calculated as: $Cov(r_S, r_B) = \rho \cdot \sigma_S \cdot \sigma_B$.
  • $r_f$: The Risk-Free Rate of return.
  • $S_P$: The Sharpe Ratio, measuring reward per unit of volatility.

1. Expected Return of the Portfolio ($E(r_P)$)

The expected return of a two-asset portfolio is simply the weighted average of the expected returns of the individual assets:

$$E(r_P) = w_S E(r_S) + w_B E(r_B)$$

2. Portfolio Risk (Variance & Standard Deviation)

Unlike return, portfolio risk is not a simple weighted average. It depends heavily on how the assets move in relation to one another (covariance). Diversification benefits arise when $\rho < 1$.

$$\sigma_P^2 = w_S^2 \sigma_S^2 + w_B^2 \sigma_B^2 + 2w_S w_B Cov(r_S, r_B)$$ $$\sigma_P = \sqrt{\sigma_P^2}$$

3. Minimum-Variance Portfolio

The specific combination of assets that yields the lowest possible overall portfolio variance. Graphically, this is the leftmost point on the investment opportunity set.

$$w_S = \frac{\sigma_B^2 - Cov(r_S, r_B)}{\sigma_S^2 + \sigma_B^2 - 2Cov(r_S, r_B)}$$

4. Optimal Risky Portfolio (Tangency Portfolio)

This is the portfolio that yields the highest possible Sharpe Ratio. The Capital Allocation Line (CAL) drawn from the risk-free rate through this portfolio has the steepest possible slope, representing the best possible risk-return trade-off.

$$S_P = \frac{E(r_P) - r_f}{\sigma_P}$$
$$w_S = \frac{[E(r_S) - r_f]\sigma_B^2 - [E(r_B) - r_f]Cov(r_S, r_B)}{[E(r_S) - r_f]\sigma_B^2 + [E(r_B) - r_f]\sigma_S^2 - [E(r_S) - r_f + E(r_B) - r_f]Cov(r_S, r_B)}$$
